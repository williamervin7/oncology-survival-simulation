# Covariate Selection Rationale

**Scope note:** Covariates are restricted to information available at or near diagnosis (baseline), consistent with treating the Cox/RSF models as baseline prognostic models for the downstream Markov microsimulation. Post-diagnosis treatment variables are considered but excluded from the primary model — see Chemotherapy below.

## Included in model

| Variable | Type | Rationale | Key citation(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | continuous | Repeatedly identified as an independent prognostic factor for OS in stage III colon/CRC cohorts, including SEER-based multivariable Cox and ML models; effect magnitude is stage-dependent, supporting inclusion rather than assumption of a fixed effect across stages. | Zhang et al. (2025), *Front. Oncol.* — SEER stage III CRC ML/Cox study, age identified as independent prognostic factor (13,855 pts); [Stage-dependent prognostic impact of age in colorectal cancer: A population-based SEER analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13542266/), *PMC13542266* |
| Sex | binary | Sex shows a consistent, if modest, independent association with CRC survival in large population-based cohorts after adjusting for stage and other covariates; direction and magnitude vary by age/stage, motivating inclusion as an adjustment covariate rather than exclusion. | Jansen et al. (2013), [Sex Differences in Colorectal Cancer Survival: Population-Based Analysis of 164,996 Colorectal Cancer Patients in Germany](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3702575/), *PLOS ONE* |
| Stage (IIIA/B/C) | categorical | Core staging variable; AJCC 7th-edition substaging was explicitly evaluated against SEER survival data and shown to separate prognostic groups, though sub-stage ordering (IIIA vs IIIB vs IIIC) is not always monotonic — directly relevant to the staging-paradox finding in this project. | Hari et al. (2013), [AJCC Cancer Staging Manual 7th Edition Criteria for Colon Cancer: Do the Complex Modifications Improve Prognostic Assessment?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23768788/), *J Am Coll Surg* (SEER, n=157,588) |
| Chemotherapy | **excluded from primary model** | Chemotherapy is a post-diagnosis exposure, not a baseline covariate. Including it in a baseline prognostic model risks immortal-time bias (patients must survive long enough to receive treatment) and treatment-selection bias (healthier patients are more likely to receive chemo), both of which would distort the Cox/RSF comparison this project is built around. Retained here only as a documented, deliberate exclusion — not a candidate for re-inclusion. | Suissa S. (2008), [Immortal Time Bias in Pharmacoepidemiology](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article-abstract/167/4/492/233064), *Am J Epidemiol* 167(4):492–499; NCI PDQ, [Colon Cancer Treatment](https://www.cancer.gov/types/colorectal/hp/colon-treatment-pdq) (adjuvant chemotherapy as post-resection standard-of-care decision, not a baseline patient characteristic) |
| Sidedness | binary | Right vs. left tumor location is an independent prognostic factor in colon cancer overall, but a large SEER propensity-matched analysis found the sidedness effect **attenuates specifically in stage III** (HR ≈ 1.0) relative to stages I–II — a stage-III-specific finding that justifies including it while flagging that its effect direction may differ from earlier-stage literature. | Warschkow et al. (2016), [Better survival in right-sided versus left-sided stage I–III colon cancer patients](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4964057/), *PMC4964057* (SEER, n=91,416, propensity-matched) |
| Marital status | categorical | Shown to be an independent prognostic factor for CRC survival within each TNM stage (including stage III) in a large SEER cohort-stratified analysis, with widowed patients consistently at highest risk — supports inclusion as a within-stage covariate rather than a stage-confounded proxy. | Li et al. (2015), [The influence of marital status on stage at diagnosis and survival of patients with colorectal cancer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4466689/), *Oncotarget* (SEER) |
| Race | categorical | Independent survival disparities by race persist in SEER-based, surgically-resected CRC cohorts after adjustment for stage, age, sex, and marital status. Underlying mechanism (access/socioeconomic vs. biological) remains actively debated and is not resolved by this covariate alone — included as an adjustment variable, not a causal claim. | Kagabo et al./Idris et al. (2022), [Racial Disparities in Survival Outcomes of Colorectal Cancer Patients After Surgical Resection](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8916922/), *PMC8916922* (SEER 2010–2016, surgical resection cohort) |
| Regional nodes examined | continuous | Nodal yield is an independent survival predictor distinct from N-stage itself: a national cohort study found the survival gap between low (0–5) and high (≥18) lymph node yield is **largest in stage III disease specifically** (36.4% vs. 59.4% 5-yr OS), supporting its inclusion as a marker of surgical/pathologic quality independent of nodal status. | Kroijer et al. (2019), [Lymph node yield and tumour subsite are associated with survival in stage I–III colon cancer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6446268/), *PMC6446268* (Danish Colorectal Cancer Group, n=13,766) |
| Year of diagnosis | continuous (or binned era) | Baseline field (known at diagnosis) already in the extract. Colorectal cancer survival has shown a documented secular improvement over time tied to treatment-era changes; large SEER-based models routinely adjust for year of diagnosis alongside age/sex/race to isolate this trend from other covariate effects. Particularly relevant here since this cohort's 2010–2023 window spans three different AJCC/EOD staging conventions. **Note:** treat the PH assumption for this covariate as a required check, not optional — calendar-time effects are a common source of non-proportional hazards (e.g., step changes around guideline updates) rather than a smooth linear trend. | Rutter et al. (2013), [Secular Trends in Colon and Rectal Cancer Relative Survival](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24174654/), *JNCI* 105(23):1806–1813 (SEER, n=233,965; model explicitly adjusts for sex, race, age, and year of diagnosis) |

## Considered, not included

| Variable | Reason excluded | Key citation(s) |
|---|---|---|
| RX Summ Surg Prim Site | Near-zero variance in resected cohort by design (cohort defined on resection status); used for cohort definition, not as a model covariate. | — (cohort-definition rationale, not a literature claim) |
| Radiation recode | Adjuvant radiation has no established standard-of-care role in resected colon cancer (unlike rectal cancer), so its use is highly selected/confounded by indication (e.g., T4/unresectable margins) rather than a general baseline risk factor. | NCI PDQ, [Colon Cancer Treatment](https://www.cancer.gov/types/colorectal/hp/colon-treatment-pdq) — "Adjuvant radiation therapy has no current standard role in the management of patients with colon cancer following curative resection"; Kim et al., [Surgery with and without adjuvant radiotherapy is associated with similar survival in T4 colon cancer](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/surgery-with-and-without-adjuvant-radiotherapy-is-associated-with/) (NCDB/SEER, propensity-matched, no significant OS difference) |
| Histologic type (mucinous/signet) | Evidence on independent prognostic effect is mixed/subtype-and-stage-dependent (protective in some sidedness/stage strata, harmful in others), and cell counts for signet-ring specifically are low in a single-stage SEER extract — flagged to revisit if n permits stratified analysis. | Ceelen W. et al. (2012), [Mucinous subtype as prognostic factor in colorectal cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis](https://ar.iiarjournals.org/content/40/12/7127) (cited in Anticancer Res 40:7127), *J Clin Pathol* 65:381–388 |
| Grade/differentiation | Well-established as a stage-independent prognostic factor in CRC multivariate analyses, but **not present in the current SEER extract** — flagged as a limitation and candidate for re-pull rather than an evidence-based exclusion. | Vather et al., [Tumour differentiation grade is associated with TNM staging and the risk of node metastasis in colorectal cancer](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.3109/02841860903334411), *Acta Oncologica* — "tumour differentiation grade is... a stage-independent prognostic factor in colorectal cancer" |
| T/N/M component fields, Regional nodes positive, SEER historic stage A | Collinear with the derived Stage (IIIA/B/C) variable already in the model; including both would not add independent information and would violate Cox model assumptions around collinearity. | — (methodological/collinearity rationale, not a literature claim) |
